A man who introduced himself as Stanimir welcomed us to the Vistani camp.

Madam Eva remarked that she once had a pet raven called “Turo”, and that our temporary companion reminded her of him. She offered to read our fortunes, but warned that a shadow was approaching the tent. We were to be careful to reveal nothing to them, not even that we knew their true nature or their master.

Then she drew five cards from her deck and read them for us.

1. Of history, for knowledge of the ancient will help against our enemy: **Traitor, nine of coins (priest)**, a guardsman with a dagger behind his back. An ancient foe of an old and noble house. A wandering soul will lead us to him.
2. At the top of the cross, a powerful force of good and protection: **Myrmidon, five of swords**, an armored warrior with sword and shield, his face hidden by a heavy helmet.
3. A burst of white, power and strength, a weapon of vengeance, the sword of sunlight: **Bishop, eight of glyphs**.
4. A light on the one who will help us greatly in the battle: **Mists (crown)**.
5. Our enemy is a creature of darkness whose power is beyond mortality. When we are ready for judgment, this card will lead us to him: **Marionette (crown)**, an emperor in finery reaching for a goblet of wine.

Madam Eva looked tired after the reading, and the flaps of the tent opened on their own. Strahd leaves her alone because he fears her: he cannot see into the future as she can. If she read our fortune again, she said, it might change our future.

**Traitor**: Look for a wealthy woman, a staunch ally of the devil. Lock and key with the bones of an ancient enemy.

**Myrmidon**: Look for a den of wolves in the hills overlooking a mountain lake. The treasure belongs to Mother Night.

**Bishop**: What you seek lies in a pile of treasure, beyond a set of amber doors.

**Mists**: A Vistana wanders alone, searching for a mentor. She does not stay in one place for long. Seek her out at Saint Markovia’s Abbey, near the mists.

**Marionette**: Look to great heights. Find the beating heart of the castle.

Just outside the tent we met Eliza and Arturi Vedonavich.

Arturi seemed to be a visitor to the camp like us, though he looked Vistani himself. It had been a while, he said, since he had sat at a Vistani fire. We were treated to a hearty meal. Eliza was very interested in our names and our journey. Arturi asked after Rudolf van Richten and claimed to know him. He is, I gather, impressively hard to kill.

I fear Eliza may be part of the shadow we were warned about.

Around the fire we told tales and wagered on who told the truth and who lied, everyone putting something up.

Stanimir went first. His people came from an ancient land of kings but were driven from it. A wounded soldier once came into their camp and they helped him. He was hunted by his enemies, and by wolves, and he claimed to be a prince. They helped him home, and he promised they would be safe in his land and welcome to come and go as they wished. Then a curse fell on the prince and turned him into a tyrant. They need heroes to help put his soul to rest.

Grom carries a tooth with him, as does every member of his family. How grim. He would probably get along with the Hexblood.

Olfin told a lie about his wretched birth and being raised by hags. Who knows whether a Hexblood lies about the lie, or what truth can come out of one.

Aera told a sad tale of her family and her loss that brought us all to tears.

Eliza sang that each raven holds a lost soul, and of Luda, of the Morninglord, of raven kind. I could not catch it all: a demon lord, Ludana perhaps, banished; a memory; a token of raven kind. The key, I think, is a **Holy Symbol of Ravenkind**.

Arturi told of a fox known for wisdom and skill. The sparrows brought him their child, but he could not cure it. Mad with grief, they stole the fox’s kit and delivered it to the wolf. The rats despised the sparrows, so the fox promised to help the rats find them to kill. The whole flock suffered for the crime of six.

Irena told a tale of a wolf near a lake, but claimed it was just a dream.

Grom won the game and the prizes: a manuscript on werewolves, a telescope that can see around corners, and a deck of special cards.

Olfin read the manuscript to us around the campfire. It was written by Dr. Rudolph van Richten and titled “A Study of the Werewolf’s Curse”:

> Werewolves are among the most fearsome lycanthropes, bearing a curse as ancient as it is terrifying. For them, the affliction of lycanthropy transforms even the most civilized individual into a monstrous beast, warping the lines betwixt man and nature.

> In its humanoid form, a werewolf retains many of the characteristics of its original identity, save for certain nuances such as heightened senses, an explosive temper, and an odd preference for rare meats. Over time, subtle features that hint at their animalistic nature begin to manifest. Nevertheless, it is in their wolf and hybrid forms that the true horror of the curse is revealed. A werewolf’s hybrid form is particularly terrifying, with a muscular humanoid body crowned by the head of a ravenous wolf. It is capable of wielding weapons, though its favored means of attack are its devastating claws and powerful bite.

> Werewolves typically abandon civilization soon after their transformation. Those who reject the curse flee in fear of harming their loved ones, whereas those who accept it fear exposure and the repercussions of their violent deeds. Out in the wild, they form packs, living in cohesion with ordinary wolves and dire wolves alike.  
> The most tragic aspect of lycanthropy is its transmission. A humanoid can contract this curse through a wound inflicted by a lycanthrope or through inheritance if one or both of its parents are lycanthropes.

> The cursed have two paths: they may resist the beast within or embrace it. Those who resist bear unending strain—until the rising full moon triggers a compulsory and horrific transformation. These individuals often experience bloody, haunting dreams: echoes of the carnage wrought in their madness.

> Some twisted souls, however, choose to accept their beastly nature. With time, they can master their abilities, calling upon the wolf’s resilience and strength as they will. To do so, however, they must first perform a particular gruesome and foul act—one which I will decline to describe in these pages.

> One sure way to identify an afflicted individual is the presence of a perpetually raw, bloody wound: the scar of the initial curse transmission. This wound never fully heals until the curse is lifted.

> Beyond this mark, however, these creatures bear an extraordinary resilience. Conventional methods of harm prove ineffective in permanently subduing a werewolf. Only through the bite of silver or the chill of death’s power can a werewolf be truly put to rest.

> A spell of removal can also cure an afflicted lycanthrope, though those who accept the beast may fight it bitterly. As for those unfortunate souls born under the curse, they are doomed to bear their affliction for life. As far as my research has shown, there exists no cure, no respite for such individuals. They are caught in an eternal struggle, forever haunted by the wolf within.

Arturi bade us farewell and walked out of the camp into the darkness.

No one in the camp was troubled by his leaving. It sounds as though he keeps away from the camp because he is chased by creatures that would hurt the innocent.

Olfin and Weeble crept after him into the forest and found him up a tree, surrounded by twelve zombies.

Later Stanimir told us Arturi’s true story. It began twenty years ago, and it is the fox’s story. Arturi searches for van Richten to learn whether his curse can be lifted.

We slept the night at the camp and healed.

In the morning Madam Eva gave Aera a pouch of ten gold pieces and asked her to take the coins to Blinsky’s toy shop in Vallaki and buy something for her grandchild, Arabelle, then deliver it to the Vistani encampment southwest of Vallaki. It must be done within two days. Arabelle is interesting to talk to, she said, and befriending her family will be a gift in itself.

On the path out of the camp we met a skeletal rider on a war horse, coming out of the mists. Perhaps it was the poor soul we found dead by the roadside, caught in the loop of the mists.
